Namaste,

Sorry for the late reply. I do not know if any Acharya before Shri Vijayindraru quoted Rama Tapaniya Upanishat. In his Chakra Mimamsa, he quotes the following list of authentic Upanishads (excluding the 10 popular upanishads, MahaNarayanopanishat, Kaushitaki, Shvetashvatara and Subalopanishat):

  1. brahmabindu
  2. tApaniya (The Narasimha Tapaniya seems to be referred as just tApaniya by Acharyas then)
  3. maitreya
  4. Jabala
  5. Kalagnirudra
  6. atharva-shikhA
  7. atharva-shiraH
  8. MahopaniShat
  9. Atmabodha
  10. mantra
  11. brahmopaniShat
  12. AraNyopaniShat
  13. Paramahamsa
  14. hamsa
  15. gAruDa
  16. rAmopaniShat (the one under discussion)
  17. GargopaniShat (or is this GarbhopaniShat)
  18. ShaariiropaniShat Then, He says there are many khila Upanishads - एवं खिलोपनिषदो अपि अनेकप्रकाराः. But he does not name them.

In turiya-shiva-khaNDanam, Shri Vijayindra quotes kaivalyopaniShat too. Shri Chalari Acharya quotes VaasudevopaniShat. Probably these are khilopanishats.

Shri Raghavendra tirtha says, in his Parimala (in PrakrityadhikaraNa 1.4), that there are 32 Upanishads. Perhaps he was referring to the above list given by his Parama-guru Shri Vijayindra tirtha.

I don’t think any of them accepted muktikaa or any other list. Dara Shikoh’s list of 60 upanishads too does not mention any such muktika / upanishad list compiler. There is quite some evidence that upanishads like muktika, sharabha, bahvRcha (which is different from Aitareya - on which there is supposedly a commentary from Appayya Dikshita) are all fake ones / later creations.

Regards, Krishna

श्री रामाय नमः. Namaskara, all glories to Shri Rama.

The following is an extract from a couple of paragraphs from Shri Vijayindra tirtha’s turiiya-shiva-khaNDanam. In that work, he examines the purvapakSha that the Trimurtis (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva) are part / inferior to the Highest Being (Sadashiva), and / or that Umapati Shiva (Parvati’s husband) is that Sadashiva himself.

Shri Vijayindra quotes the following parts of Rama-tapaniya-upaniShat (both Purva and Uttara parts) in his work:

Shri Rama gives this boon to Rudra:

अविमुक्ते तव क्षेत्रे सर्वेषां मुक्तिसिद्धये | अहं सन्निहितः तत्र पाषाणप्रतिमादिषु ||

In your Kshetra called Avimukta (another name for Kashi), I will reside in all objects - stones to idols - to enable people get mokSha.

But how can one get moksha without jnaana, just by dying in Kashi? Well, Rudra gives him the ’taaraka mantra'.

अत्र हि जन्तोः प्राणेषु उत्क्रममाणेषु रुद्रः तारकं ब्रह्म व्याचष्टे येनासौ अमुती भूत्वा मोक्षी भवति

Indeed here, when a being’s praanas are rising up (to leave), Rudra tells the taraka mantra to that being, by which that being is liberated from his sins and eventually becomes mukta.

But what is this taaraka mantra? The 6-lettered mantra on Shri Rama, which is further detailed in the RamopaniShat. Towards the end, Shri Rama instructs Rudra:

त्वत्तो वा ब्रह्मणो वापि ये लभन्ते षडक्षरं जीवन्तो मन्त्रसिद्धाः स्युः मुक्ता मां प्राप्नुवन्ति ते || मुमूर्षोर्दक्षिणे कर्णे यस्य कस्यापि वा स्वयम् | उपदेक्ष्यसि मन्मन्त्रं स मुक्तः भविता शिव ||

Those who, when alive, get the 6-syllable-mantra from you or Chaturmukha Brahma, and accomplish the powers of that mantra (i.e. get mantra-siddhi), will become liberated and reach Me. If you yourself give upadesha of my mantra to any person, whosoever, on his death-bed in his right ear, he will become a mukta.

Points to note:

  1. Rudra does Rama-mantra-japa, homa etc., in Kashi, for a thousand ‘manvantaras’! I think the manvantara-word here refers to some other time-duration, not the regular manvantara.

  2. It is conditional. Not everyone who dies in Kashi gets mokSha. Only if Rudra gives the upadesha of Rama mantra, one gets mokSha.

  3. Shrimad Anandatirtha Bhagavatpadacharya says in his Gitabhashya 3.20 - if any verse refers to any means to liberation without jnaana or aparoxa, such as dying in Prayaga, the scope of such liberation would be limited to liberation from sins (not from samsaara). Or such is the means to getting knowledge - which will eventually lead to mokSha.

Keeping that in mind, one can notice that the above mokSha is available for one who, when living, is eligible to get upadesha from Rudra! Such must be a uttama adhikaari. It is quite likely that such people get this upadesha after their aparoxa. Therefore the rule ‘knowledge is the only direct means to mokSha’ is not compromised.

For other people (mentioned as ‘yasya kasyaapi’ in above line), as Acharya says, such statements have limited application. They get freed from their sins, and become eligible for knowledge.

  1. The above reading is different from the available version of Rama-tapaniya-upanishat. I trust Shri Vijayindra’s version over the extant version, which seems to derived from the advaitic commentary from BrahmendraYogi (whose versions don’t match that of his own predecessors). Anyway, the above point is available even in the extant version.

श्रीमध्वेशरामार्पणमस्तु
